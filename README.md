## Project Summary 
I created an SNHU Travel homepage component featuring a list of top wellness destinations. 

Each week I acted like a different Scrum team member (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Tester, Developer) in an agile environment, delivering functional outputs towards the Sprint goal, and journaling my experiences, lessons learned, wins, and opportunities for improvement. At the end of the work, I created a retrospective document to summarize these outputs (CS-250_M7_Retrospective_JDAmico.docx).

Lessons:
- one source of truth for the user stories and requirements
- open and transparent communication with each expert team member
- the ability to test and fix things as we went along instead of at the end
- leveraging the Scrum Master to help remove impediments and the Product Owner to funnel any mid-stream requirement changes


## Questions
### Q1: How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
The Product Owner prioritizes project requirements and orders user stories for the Product Backlog. As a developer, I read through the user stories which contain plain language descriptions of software features (As a [user], I want [goal] so that [reason]), from the perspective of the end users. This makes a feature fit into the why and how of the larger picture, and helps me deliver what the end user expects with the end product. During Sprint Planning, developers sort through the user stories and select which will be broken down into further smaller pieces, then add these tasks to the Sprint backlog. Based on the Sprint Backlog user stories, the Testers create Test Cases for the functionality and requirements, and test each deliverable from the developers. The developers and testers work hand-in-hand to resolve defects prior to the end of the Sprint.

***Example:*** In the SNHU Travel project, both the Testers and Developers read through the User Stories to understand why we needed to change the user profiles to add destination preferences, which later helped the development work and testing work.


### Q2: How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to use in my future development work?
In a Scrum team, I need to collectively develop programs with my fellow team members. I need to break the requirements down into more manageable tasks and actively collaborate with the Testers and address defects based on Test Case results. As a team we need to output a fully functional increment at the end of the Sprint. Being able to develop work and test it immediately provides continuous improvement rather than a rush test at the end of the project, like with Waterfall, and possibly not delivering a fully functional end product. The Agile processes that I hope to use in my future development are Sprint planning, testing alongside development, and incremental outputs that are fully functional and can be integrated into a larger product at the end of the series of Sprints.

***Example:*** In the SNHU Travel project, the team had to pivot after a major scope change. We leveraged an emergency planning meeting after the Product Owner decided to scrap the current Sprint and work the new Scope into a new Sprint. The team stepped up in each of their areas to make the new requirements a part of the work in flight.


### Q3: What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
A good team member is capable, knows who to reach out to, communicates frequently and is responsive, adapts to changing requirements, and welcomes the opportunity to continuously improve both their own approach and the product increment they’re working on. The Developer and Tester roles helped me understand the importance of open and continuous communication, both with each other and the Product Owner.

***Example:*** In the SNHU Travel project, two team members reached out to the Product Owner to obtain clarity on the new Scope and what it meant to the functionality and look of the newly revised component. To save time, both used filler which was easily finalized later.
